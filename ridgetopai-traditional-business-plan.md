# RidgeTop AI: Traditional Business Structure Reference

## Purpose

This document outlines how a traditional mid-sized software company (100-500 employees) is structured. This serves as the "before" snapshot—the established model that has worked for decades.

The goal: Use this as a foundation for SIRK instances to reimagine how a **solo builder with AI** can achieve equivalent output across each of these functions.

---

## Company Profile: The Traditional Mid-Sized Software Company

- **Size**: 100-500 employees
- **Revenue**: $10M-$100M ARR
- **Stage**: Established, profitable, sustainable
- **Products**: Multiple software products, likely B2B SaaS
- **Not**: A startup burning VC money. Not a tech giant with unlimited resources.

This is the "middle ground"—companies that have figured out product-market fit, have paying customers, and just churn along with a solid business model.

---

## Executive Leadership

At this size, you typically see a formalized C-suite:

### CEO (Chief Executive Officer)
- Overall vision and strategy
- Board relations and investor communication
- External-facing leadership and representation
- Sets company culture and values
- Final decision-maker on major initiatives

### CTO (Chief Technology Officer)
- Technology strategy and R&D direction
- Technical architecture decisions
- Owns the engineering organization
- Evaluates and adopts new technologies
- Balances technical debt vs. feature development

### CFO (Chief Financial Officer)
- Financial planning and budgeting
- Risk management and compliance
- Investor relations and reporting
- Cash flow management
- M&A evaluation

### COO (Chief Operating Officer) - Sometimes Present
- Day-to-day operations oversight
- Process optimization
- Cross-functional coordination
- Resource allocation
- Scaling operations

### Other C-Level Roles (as company grows)
- **CPO** (Chief Product Officer) - Product vision and roadmap
- **CRO** (Chief Revenue Officer) - Sales and revenue growth
- **CMO** (Chief Marketing Officer) - Brand and demand generation
- **CHRO** (Chief Human Resources Officer) - People and culture

## Department 1: Engineering / Product Development

**Purpose**: Build and maintain the software products that generate revenue.

**Typical headcount**: 30-50% of total company

### Sub-Teams

#### Frontend Development
- Implement user interfaces
- Ensure responsive, accessible design
- Optimize performance and load times
- Work closely with UX/UI designers

#### Backend Development
- Build APIs and business logic
- Manage data layer and databases
- Ensure scalability and reliability
- Integration with third-party services

#### DevOps / Platform Engineering
- CI/CD pipeline management
- Infrastructure as code
- Cloud resource management (AWS, GCP, Azure)
- Monitoring, logging, alerting
- Security automation

#### Quality Assurance (QA)
- Manual testing for edge cases
- Automated test suite development
- Regression testing
- Performance and load testing
- Bug triage and tracking

#### Security Engineering
- Application security reviews
- Vulnerability scanning and remediation
- Security architecture
- Compliance (SOC 2, GDPR, etc.)
- Incident response

### Key Roles
- VP of Engineering
- Engineering Managers
- Software Architects
- Tech Leads
- Senior/Mid/Junior Developers
- QA Engineers (manual + automation)
- DevOps/SRE Engineers
- Security Engineers

### Key Metrics
- Deployment frequency
- Lead time for changes
- Mean time to recovery (MTTR)
- Bug escape rate
- Code coverage
- System uptime

## Key Ratios and Benchmarks

### Headcount Distribution (typical)
- Engineering: 30-50%
- Sales & Marketing: 20-30%
- Customer Success & Support: 10-15%
- G&A (Finance, HR, Legal, IT, Ops): 10-15%

### Spending as % of ARR (bootstrapped companies)
- Sales: ~13%
- Marketing: ~8%
- R&D: ~25-35%
- Customer Success: ~8%
- G&A: ~15-20%

### Engineering to Sales Ratio
- Early stage: 2:1 (more engineers)
- Growth stage: 1:1 (balanced)
- Scale stage: Can shift toward more GTM

## What This Structure Accomplishes

1. **Specialization** - Each function has dedicated experts
2. **Clear ownership** - Every outcome has a responsible party
3. **Scalability** - Can add headcount to any function as needed
4. **Risk distribution** - No single point of failure
5. **Quality control** - Multiple checkpoints and reviews
6. **Institutional knowledge** - Expertise accumulated over time
7. **Career paths** - Clear progression for employees

## The Challenge for Solo Builders

A solo builder cannot hire 200 people. But they still need:

- Products that get built and work
- Customers who find and buy the product
- Support when things break
- Legal protection
- Financial management
- Security and compliance
- Marketing and brand presence
- Documentation and training

**The question**: How does a solo builder with AI achieve the *output* of this structure without the *headcount*?
